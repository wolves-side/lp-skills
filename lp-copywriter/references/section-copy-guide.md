# Section Copy Guide

Specific guidance for writing each standard LP section. Sections correspond to
the recommended structure in the Master Brief (Part F).

---

## Section 1: Hero

**Job:** Stop the scroll. Communicate the core value in under 5 seconds.
**Framework:** USP-First
**Word budget:** Headline 6-12 words, subheadline 12-20 words.

**Write 3 headline variants using different angles:**
- **Variant A (Result):** Lead with the outcome/number ("Cut costs by 40% in 90 days")
- **Variant B (Disqualifier):** Exclude to attract ("Most X do Y. We do Z.")
- **Variant C (Mechanism):** Lead with the unique approach ("The first AI system that...")

**Subheadline must answer:** For whom? How? In what timeframe?

**Above-the-fold trust anchor:** ONE proof element.
Choose the strongest available: client count, revenue metric, logo bar, or micro-quote.

**CTA rules:**
- Verb + specific benefit: "Get Your Free AI Analysis" not "Get Started"
- Microcopy below CTA: remove friction ("Free • 15 minutes • No commitment")
- If LP type is Service Sale: CTA = scheduling action
- If LP type is Lead Capture: CTA = download/access action

## Section 2: Problem Agitation

**Job:** Make the reader feel understood. Amplify the pain they already feel.
**Framework:** PAS (Problem → Agitation only. Solution comes next section.)
**Word budget:** 80-150 words.

**Writing approach:**
- Open with their EXACT words (Customer Language Bank, problem words)
- Describe a day-in-the-life scenario from the Product Brief
- Escalate: "And that means..." → consequence → bigger consequence
- End on the cliff: transition phrase that implies a solution exists

**Tone:** Empathetic, not condescending. You understand because you've seen it, not because you think they're stupid.

**Do NOT:**
- Mention your product/company yet
- Offer the solution yet (that's next section)
- Use more than 3 sentences of agitation (don't overdo it)

---

## Section 3: Solution Reveal

**Job:** Introduce your approach as the answer to the pain you just amplified.
**Framework:** BAB (Before-After-Bridge) or direct mechanism reveal.
**Word budget:** 100-200 words.

**Writing approach:**
- Bridge from the problem: "What if..." or "There's a reason this keeps happening..."
- Reveal the MECHANISM, not the product. Why does your approach work?
- Paint the AFTER state: mirror the BEFORE from previous section, now resolved
- Include timeframe: "In [X weeks/days]..."

**Critical rule:** The mechanism explanation must be SIMPLE.
If a 14-year-old can't understand the explanation, simplify it.

---

## Section 4: Social Proof / Results

**Job:** Prove the claims with evidence. Shift from "trust me" to "see for yourself."
**Framework:** Proof Stack
**Word budget:** Varies by proof type.

**Adapt to what's AVAILABLE (from Master Brief Part B, Trust Signals):**

| Available | Write |
|-----------|-------|
| Named testimonials with photos | Full quote blocks with attribution |
| Anonymous but real results | "A [industry] company with [X] employees..." case summaries |
| Numbers only (no names) | Metrics grid: "23 clients • R$500K revenue • 6 industries" |
| No proof at all | Skip or use founder's own metrics/experience as proof |

**Case study format (if writing mini-cases):**
```
[TITLE: Result-focused headline]
Challenge: [1 sentence — what they struggled with]
Solution: [1 sentence — what you did]
Result: [1 sentence — measurable outcome with numbers]
Timeline: [How long it took]
```

**NEVER fabricate testimonials or results.** Use what exists. Flag gaps.

## Section 5: Features / What's Included

**Job:** Show what the prospect actually gets. Translate features into outcomes.
**Framework:** Feature-Benefit-Outcome (FBO)
**Word budget:** 30-50 words per feature block, 3-6 features.

**Writing approach:**
- Each feature is a card/block with: outcome headline + benefit description + feature detail
- Lead with OUTCOME, not feature name
- Use icons or emojis as visual anchors in the copy (the designer will replace with real icons)

**Structure per feature:**
```
[OUTCOME HEADLINE] (what it means for them)
[1-2 sentences: benefit + how the feature delivers it]
```

**Example:**
```
Never chase a report again
Our AI pipeline delivers formatted reports to your inbox by 7am.
Connects to your existing systems — no migration, no disruption.
```

## Section 6: Comparison / Value Anchor

**Job:** Make the price feel small by comparing to expensive alternatives.
**Framework:** Direct comparison (us vs. them / us vs. alternative)
**Word budget:** 80-120 words or comparison table.

**Format options:**
- **Table format:** Us vs Enterprise vs DIY — feature comparison
- **Narrative format:** "Enterprise consultancies charge $X for this. We deliver the same result for $Y because [reason]."
- **Cost-of-inaction format:** "Every month without this costs you $X in [waste/lost revenue]."

**Use the anchoring data from the Product Brief** (Section 4, Price Anchoring).

**Rule:** The comparison must be FAIR. Don't strawman competitors.
Show genuine advantages, acknowledge where you differ (scope, team size, etc.)

## Section 7: Testimonials

**Job:** Let other people sell for you. Social validation through peer voices.
**Framework:** Proof Stack (Layer 3)
**Word budget:** 20-40 words per testimonial.

**If real testimonials exist:**
```
"[Exact quote — keep it short, impactful, specific]"
— [Full name], [Title], [Company]
```

**If only anonymous feedback:**
```
"[Paraphrased result-focused statement]"
— [Title], [Industry] company, [Size]
```

**If NO testimonials exist:**
Skip this section entirely. Do NOT fabricate.
Suggest to the client: "Get 3 LinkedIn recommendations this week."

**Curation rules:**
- Select quotes that address different objections (price, trust, results, ease)
- Favor quotes with NUMBERS over quotes with feelings
- Shorter is better: edit for clarity (with attribution maintained)

## Section 8: Offer Block

**Job:** Present the complete offer. Price + guarantee + urgency. Decision time.
**Framework:** Risk Reversal + Urgency
**Word budget:** 100-200 words.

**Structure:**
```
1. WHAT YOU GET (restate core offer + any bonuses)
2. PRICE (if applicable — with anchoring context)
3. GUARANTEE (make this the loudest element)
4. URGENCY (if real — capacity, deadline, price increase)
5. CTA (repeated from hero, possibly stronger variant)
6. MICROCOPY (risk removers: "Cancel anytime", "No lock-in", etc.)
```

**For Service Sale LPs (no price shown):**
Replace price with value statement: "Valued at $X — complimentary for qualified companies."

**Guarantee copy must be:**
- Specific (what exactly happens if they're not satisfied)
- Time-bound (within what period)
- Unconditional or clearly conditional (state the terms)

## Section 9: FAQ

**Job:** Handle remaining objections disguised as helpful answers.
**Framework:** Acknowledge-Reframe-Prove
**Word budget:** 40-80 words per Q&A, 5-8 questions.

**Mandatory questions (from Objection Map in Master Brief):**
Each mapped objection becomes a FAQ question.

**Additional strategic questions:**
- "How long until I see results?" (reinforces timeframe promise)
- "What makes you different from [competitor type]?" (reinforces positioning)
- "What if it doesn't work?" (reinforces guarantee)
- "How do I get started?" (removes friction, explains next step)

**Writing rules:**
- Question phrased in FIRST PERSON ("How long will it take?") not third ("How long does it take?")
- Answer starts with the direct answer, THEN elaborates
- End each answer with a mini-CTA or transition to action when natural

---

## Section 10: Final CTA

**Job:** Last chance to convert. Summarize + push.
**Framework:** Summary + Risk Reversal
**Word budget:** 50-80 words.

**Structure:**
```
H2: [Restate the transformation in one powerful line]
BODY: [1-2 sentences summarizing: what they get + guarantee + urgency]
CTA: [Strongest CTA variant — potentially different from hero]
MICROCOPY: [Final risk remover]
```

**This section should feel like the CONCLUSION, not a repeat.**
New angle or emotional note, not copy-paste from hero.

---

## Section 11: Trust Footer

**Job:** Reassure the cautious prospect who scrolled all the way down.
**Framework:** Proof Stack (supporting layers)
**Word budget:** 50-100 words.

**Elements:**
- Founder mini-bio (2-3 sentences from Founder Story)
- Technology/tool stack (what powers the solution)
- Company details (location, team, founded date)
- Contact methods (email, WhatsApp, social links)
- Guarantee reminder (one-line restatement)
- Legal (privacy policy, terms if applicable)

**Tone:** Warm, human, confident. "You're in good hands" energy.
