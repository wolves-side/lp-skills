# Copy Frameworks for Landing Pages

## When to Use Each Framework

Match the framework to the PSYCHOLOGICAL ROLE of the section, not the section name.

| Section Role | Best Framework | Why |
|-------------|---------------|-----|
| Hook / Capture attention | **USP-first** | Lead with the unique value instantly |
| Agitate the problem | **PAS** (Problem-Agitation-Solution) | Build emotional urgency before solving |
| Reveal the solution | **BAB** (Before-After-Bridge) | Show transformation with your method as bridge |
| Build credibility | **Proof Stack** | Layer evidence from weakest to strongest |
| Explain features | **Feature-Benefit-Outcome** | Connect what it IS to what it DOES to why it MATTERS |
| Handle objections | **Acknowledge-Reframe-Prove** | Don't dismiss — validate then redirect |
| Close / Drive action | **Risk Reversal + Urgency** | Remove fear, create momentum |

## Framework 1: USP-First (Hero Section)

```
HEADLINE: [Unique claim that NO competitor can make]
SUBHEADLINE: [How / for whom — adds context to the claim]
PROOF ANCHOR: [One number or trust signal that makes the claim credible]
CTA: [Specific action with implied benefit]
```

**Rules:**
- Headline: 6-12 words MAX. Every word earns its place.
- Subheadline: 12-20 words. Clarifies WHO it's for and HOW.
- Proof anchor: ONE stat, ONE logo bar, or ONE micro-testimonial.
- CTA: Verb + benefit, not just verb. "Get Your Free Analysis" not "Submit."

**Example:**
```
H1: Most AI agencies ship chatbots. We ship revenue systems.
H2: Custom AI infrastructure for Brazilian SMBs that cuts costs by 40% in 90 days.
PROOF: Trusted by 23 companies across 6 industries.
CTA: Get Your Free AI ROI Analysis →
```

## Framework 2: PAS — Problem-Agitation-Solution

Use for: Problem section, pain agitation, "why now" urgency.

```
PROBLEM: [State the problem in THEIR words — customer language]

AGITATION: [Make it worse. Show consequences of NOT solving it.
            Use specific scenarios, numbers, emotional stakes.]

SOLUTION: [Transition to your approach — NOT your product yet,
           but the PRINCIPLE that solves it.]
```

**Rules:**
- Problem: 1-2 sentences. Direct. Use exact phrases from Customer Language Bank.
- Agitation: 2-3 sentences. Escalate. Show what happens in 6 months if nothing changes.
- Solution: 1 sentence bridge. "That's why we built..." or "There's a better way."
- NEVER agitate to the point of hopelessness. The reader must feel "this is fixable."

**Example:**
```
P: Your team spends 47 hours a month copying data between spreadsheets.
A: That's 564 hours a year — the equivalent of hiring someone full-time
   just to move numbers around. While your competitors automate and scale,
   you're burning cash on work a machine should do. And every manual entry
   is another chance for an error that tanks a client relationship.
S: What if those 47 hours just... disappeared?
```

## Framework 3: BAB — Before-After-Bridge

Use for: Solution reveal, transformation sections, case studies.

```
BEFORE: [The painful current state — vivid, specific, emotional]

AFTER: [The desired future state — equally vivid, with measurable outcomes]

BRIDGE: [Your product/service as the path between them.
         This is where you explain the MECHANISM.]
```

**Rules:**
- Before and After should MIRROR each other (same dimensions, opposite outcomes).
- The Bridge explains WHY your approach works — the unique mechanism.
- Include timeframe: how fast does the transformation happen?
- Anchor with proof: who else has made this journey?

**Example:**
```
BEFORE: You're drowning in manual reports. Every Monday starts with
        3 hours of copying, formatting, and praying the numbers match.

AFTER:  Your reports generate themselves overnight. Monday starts with
        coffee and strategic decisions, not data entry.

BRIDGE: Our AI pipeline connects your systems, validates data automatically,
        and delivers formatted reports by 7am. BIC Amazônia made the switch
        in 2 weeks. Their Monday routine dropped from 3 hours to 12 minutes.
```

## Framework 4: Proof Stack

Use for: Social proof sections, trust building, credibility blocks.

Layer evidence from MOST ACCESSIBLE to MOST IMPRESSIVE:

```
LAYER 1 — Numbers (fast to scan):
[X clients] | [Y revenue generated] | [Z% average improvement]

LAYER 2 — Logos / Names (visual trust):
[Recognizable client logos or names]

LAYER 3 — Micro-testimonials (social proof):
"[Short quote]" — [Name, Title, Company]

LAYER 4 — Case study summary (deep proof):
[Client] went from [BEFORE metric] to [AFTER metric] in [timeframe].

LAYER 5 — Guarantee (risk removal):
[If we don't deliver X, you get Y.]
```

**Rules:**
- Not every LP needs all 5 layers. Use what you HAVE (from the Master Brief assets).
- NEVER fabricate. If you only have 2 layers, use 2 layers with strength.
- Layer 3 (testimonials): Use REAL quotes or don't use them at all.
- Each layer should introduce NEW information, not repeat the same claim.

## Framework 5: Feature-Benefit-Outcome (FBO)

Use for: Feature sections, "what's included", service descriptions.

For each feature:

```
FEATURE: [What it IS — technical/descriptive]
BENEFIT: [What it DOES for the user — functional]
OUTCOME: [Why it MATTERS — emotional/business impact]
```

**Rules:**
- Lead with Outcome in the headline, not Feature.
- Feature is supporting detail, not the star.
- Each FBO block should be scannable (headline + 1-2 sentences max).

**Example:**
```
OUTCOME (headline): Never miss an invoice again
BENEFIT: Automated invoice processing catches errors before they cost you
FEATURE: AI-powered OCR reads, validates, and routes 12,000+ invoices/month
```

## Framework 6: Acknowledge-Reframe-Prove (FAQ / Objections)

Use for: FAQ section, objection handling, "but what about..." blocks.

```
ACKNOWLEDGE: "You might be thinking: [exact objection in their words]"

REFRAME: [Change the frame. Show why this concern actually supports buying.]

PROVE: [Evidence that the reframe is true — case study, guarantee, data.]
```

**Rules:**
- NEVER dismiss the objection. Start with "That's a fair question" energy.
- The reframe should feel like a revelation, not a rebuttal.
- End with proof, not argument. Data > persuasion.

**Example:**
```
A: "We've tried AI solutions before and they didn't work."
R: That's actually why clients come to us. Generic AI tools fail because
   they don't understand your specific workflows. We don't sell tools —
   we build custom systems that integrate with what you already use.
P: 8 of our 23 clients came to us after a failed AI implementation elsewhere.
   Average time from "broken" to "working": 3 weeks.
```

## Framework 7: Risk Reversal + Urgency (Closing Section)

Use for: Final CTA section, offer block, closing.

```
SUMMARY: [Restate the core value in ONE sentence]

RISK REVERSAL: [Guarantee that makes "yes" safer than "no"]

URGENCY: [Real reason to act NOW, not later]

CTA: [Action + benefit + support text]
```

**Rules:**
- The guarantee should be the LOUDEST element in this section.
- Urgency must be REAL. Fake scarcity = trust destruction.
- CTA support text removes the last micro-friction ("No credit card", "Takes 2 min").
- If there's no real urgency, skip it. Don't manufacture it.

---

## Choosing the Right Framework

When writing each section, ask:

1. What is this section's JOB? (Hook? Agitate? Prove? Close?)
2. What does the reader FEEL when they arrive at this section?
3. What should they feel AFTER reading it?
4. Which framework moves them from state A to state B most efficiently?

The framework is a starting structure, not a cage.
Combine frameworks when a section serves multiple roles.
